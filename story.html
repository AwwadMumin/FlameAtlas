On Earth, fire climbs. In orbit, it waits.

Without gravity, hot gas does not rise. A flame stops reaching upward and becomes a quiet blue sphere, fed only by oxygen drifting in.

A spacecraft fire leaves nowhere to run.

  • 1967Apollo 1: fire in an oxygen-filled capsule during a pre-launch test. The crew died.
  • 1970Apollo 13: an oxygen tank exploded on the way to the Moon.
  • 1997Mir: an oxygen generator failure started a fire aboard the station.
  • 2026Artemis II: a smoke alarm went off about 80,000 miles from home.

Events as described in NASA's 2026 Flame in Freefall challenge statement.

Plotted by oxygen and pressure, all of NASA's solid-fuel burns fall between about 16 and 23 percent oxygen, at roughly Earth's sea-level pressure.

The Moon Base will not live there. NASA expects 30 percent oxygen or more, at 10.2 psi or less, to make spacewalks easier.

    The core equation, solved on real NASA data

    How Flame Atlas decides whether a NASA test counts as evidence for your habitat. No language model decides this: it is arithmetic you can check by hand.

    d = √( Σ ( (xᵢ − sᵢ) / τᵢ )² )
    xᵢ = test value · sᵢ = habitat value · τᵢ = tolerance
    τᵢ = max( 20% of the tested range , physical floor )
    d ≤ 1 → close (counts as evidence) · 1 < d ≤ 2 → far · d > 2 → ignored
